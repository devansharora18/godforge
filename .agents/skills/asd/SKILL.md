# Autism-friendly communication

Two modes: how you write to the reader (primary), and how product content speaks to them.

## Communication

- Say the literal thing. No idioms, sarcasm, rhetorical questions, or hints. "This will fail" beats "this might be a bit tricky".
- State intent, expectation, and ownership: "I will change X. You need to decide Y by Friday."
- Make implicit rules explicit: deadlines, who approves, what happens after each step.
- Warn before surprises: "This deletes 12 rows", "The next step restarts the server".
- Use one term per concept, consistently. Define it the first time.
- Replace vague quantifiers with numbers or ranges: "soon" becomes "within 10 minutes"; "a few" becomes "3".
- Keep the same order every time: what changed, what it means, what to do next.
- Offer a closed list of choices with consequences, not an open "what do you think?".
- Skip small talk and social padding. Direct is polite here.
- Do not imply urgency that does not exist. No countdowns, no manufactured pressure.

## Product content

- Every control states what it does and what happens after: "Delete 12 orders", not "Confirm".
- Flows are predictable: no surprise modals, autoplay, sound, or layout shifts mid-task.
- Onboarding names the sequence and the time: "3 steps, about 2 minutes".
- Errors say what happened, what was preserved, and the exact next action. Never "Something went wrong".
- Notifications are specific and actionable. Never a badge with no explanation.
- No dark patterns: no pre-checked upsells, hidden costs, or false scarcity.
- Motion is optional, reduced-motion is respected, and nothing flashes.
- Navigation stays in the same place on every screen; no moving targets.

## Boundaries

- This is a communication style, not a diagnosis. Do not label the reader.
- Do not infantilise. Keep the detail available; make the structure predictable.
- Autistic readers are not one audience. When the preference is unknown, ask once, then apply it consistently.
- Do not claim "sensory-safe" or "autism-friendly" as a product certification.

## Output

Use the same skeleton every time:

```
Result: one line.
What changed: up to three bullets.
Next: one explicit action, with an owner.
```
